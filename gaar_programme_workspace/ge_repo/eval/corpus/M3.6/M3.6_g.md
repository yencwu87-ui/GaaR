# Expansion Decision Pack — Digital Offer Eligibility & Prioritisation (DOEP)

Assembled by: Retail Digital Product, with Model Risk Management and Conduct Risk input
Ref: DOEP/EXP/2026-09
Decision sought: expansion from controlled deployment (8% of eligible base) to full digital base
Committee: Retail Product & Conduct Committee, 24 September 2026
Version: 1.2 (incorporating MRM comments of 11 September)

---

## 1. The decision in front of the committee

DOEP determines which existing customers see an offer for the Flexi Balance Transfer product in
the mobile app and web banking, and in what priority order when multiple offers compete for the
same placement. It does not underwrite, price, or approve: a customer who accepts an offer still
passes full credit assessment.

The model has run in controlled deployment since 11 March 2026 across a randomly selected 8% of
the eligible base (approx. 214,000 customers). The proposal is to extend to the full eligible base
(approx. 2.68m customers).

Governance classification: **Heightened**, under the AI & Advanced Analytics Governance Standard
(AAGS-2025-01 §5.2), on the basis of scale of customer impact and the consequence of
non-selection — a customer not shown an offer has no visibility of it and no route to request it.
The classification was set at intake on 4 November 2025 and re-confirmed at the controlled
deployment gate on 26 February 2026.

## 3. Model and evaluation

DOEP scores propensity to accept and expected value, combined with hard eligibility rules
(product holding, arrears status, marketing consent, vulnerability flags). The model component is
a gradient-boosted propensity model; eligibility rules are deterministic and sit outside it.

### 3.1 Pre-deployment evaluation

Evaluated on a 14-month holdout of prior balance-transfer campaign response. AUC 0.712
out-of-sample, 0.694 out-of-time. Success criteria were set in the model design note of
8 January 2026 (AUC ≥ 0.65 out-of-time; uplift ≥ 1.4x over the incumbent rules-based targeting)
and were met.

### 3.2 Representativeness — issue raised during controlled deployment

The pre-deployment evaluation population was drawn from customers who had received a
balance-transfer campaign between 2023 and 2025. MRM's February review noted at its Finding
M-02 that this population is not representative of the full eligible base, because historic
campaign selection was itself rules-based and skewed toward customers with prior card balances.

The controlled deployment was designed partly to address this. Analysis at Appendix B compares
the controlled-deployment population to the full eligible base across tenure, product holding,
income band, age band, and channel activity. Two material differences remain:

- customers with no prior revolving balance are 11.4% of the controlled population against 27.8%
  of the full eligible base
- customers aged 60+ are 6.2% against 14.1%

The controlled deployment therefore does not give the bank evidence of performance for two
substantial groups within the population it proposes to expand to. This is stated plainly here
because it bears directly on the decision.

### 3.3 Segment behaviour observed in controlled deployment

| Segment | Offer rate | Acceptance rate | Model AUC (in-segment) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prior revolving balance | 34.1% | 4.8% | 0.718 |
| No prior revolving balance | 9.6% | 2.1% | 0.611 |
| Age <40 | 29.7% | 4.4% | 0.709 |
| Age 40–59 | 26.2% | 4.1% | 0.702 |
| Age 60+ | 12.8% | 2.6% | 0.588 |
| Vulnerability flag present | 0% (excluded by rule) | — | — |

The offer-rate differences are partly a function of the eligibility rules and partly of the model.
The decomposition at Appendix C attributes approximately two-thirds of the age-60+ gap to the
rules and one-third to model score distribution.

Conduct Risk's assessment considered whether the age-band difference constitutes a fairness
concern. Its conclusion (CR/ASSESS/2026/007 §5) is that the differential is explicable by product
relevance rather than by a protected characteristic being used as a proxy, but it records that the
in-segment AUC of 0.588 for the 60+ group means the model discriminates poorly within that group,
and recommends either segment-specific treatment or exclusion of the segment pending better
evidence. That recommendation is not yet actioned.

## 4. Independent review for expansion — findings

MRM/VAL/2026/088, issued 8 September 2026. Findings:

| Ref | Finding | Severity | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| M-05 | Controlled deployment population unrepresentative on two material dimensions (§3.2); expansion would apply the model to populations on which it has not been evaluated | High | Open |
| M-06 | In-segment performance for age 60+ below the model's own out-of-time success criterion | High | Open |
| M-07 | Conduct Risk recommendation on 60+ treatment not actioned | Medium | Open |
| M-08 | Monitoring plan has no in-flight trigger for segment-level performance degradation | Medium | Owner accepted; plan revision drafted, not approved |
| M-09 | Offer suppression is not visible to the customer and there is no complaint route specific to non-selection | Medium | Referred to Conduct Risk |

MRM's stated opinion is that **expansion to the full eligible base is not supported on current
evidence**, and that expansion limited to the segments represented in the controlled deployment
would be supportable subject to M-08.

The product team's response, at Appendix E, argues that M-05 is addressed by the eligibility rules
excluding the highest-risk populations and that a staged expansion with monitoring is adequate.
MRM has not changed its opinion. The disagreement is unresolved and is put to the committee as
such.

---

## 5. What the committee is being asked to weigh

Product's recommendation: expand to full eligible base with enhanced monitoring.
MRM's opinion: do not expand beyond represented segments.
Conduct Risk's position: supports expansion for represented segments; maintains the 60+
recommendation at §3.3.

Accountable owner for the model and for residual risk arising from whichever option is chosen:
Head of Retail Digital Product. Accountable owner for conduct outcomes: Head of Conduct Risk,
Retail.

Limitations that attach to any approval:

1. The model has not been evaluated on customers with no prior revolving balance at
   representative volume, nor on customers aged 60+ at representative volume.
2. In-segment performance for 60+ is below the model's own success criterion.
3. Non-selection is invisible to the customer; no specific complaint route exists.
4. Segment-level in-flight monitoring triggers are drafted but not approved.

---

## 6. If approved

Appendix D sets out the monitoring to be in place from expansion: weekly offer-rate and
acceptance-rate by the segments at §3.3, monthly model performance, monthly complaint themes
review with Conduct Risk. The revised plan adding segment degradation triggers (M-08) requires
Model Risk Committee approval before expansion if the committee approves on that basis.

Broader lifecycle arrangements for DOEP — periodic revalidation cadence, the tiering review cycle,
and the conditions under which a further independent review is required — are held in the model
inventory record and in AAGS-2025-01, not in this pack.

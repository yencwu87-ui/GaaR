# M3.6 — executable requirement-element contract v0.9.6

The element set is now an atomic, test-oriented contract. The goal is not to maximise the number
of elements; it is to make each element independently answerable by evidence, deterministic tests,
or a tightly scoped inference pass. The element list deliberately keeps evaluation/testing,
pre-deployment review, independent validation, outcome recording and re-validation in the M3.6
boundary while excluding technology/cybersecurity and post-deployment monitoring.

## Revised 14 elements

| id | Element | Verification intent |
|---|---|---|
| e1 | Evaluation measures aligned to the AI's objectives and acceptable performance thresholds are defined and documented before testing begins. | Inspect evaluation standard/test plan and timing. |
| e2 | Evaluation and testing are performed for the specific AI use case, system or model under representative data and plausible conditions of use, with results retained. | Reconcile test identity, dataset/context and retained results. |
| e3 | The testing approach covers relevant performance, error and failure conditions, including edge cases where applicable. | Inspect test catalogue and execution evidence. |
| e4 | The testing approach includes robustness, security or adversarial testing where those risks are relevant. | Confirm applicability and test coverage; do not demand irrelevant tests. |
| e5 | The testing approach considers sub-population, fairness or other stakeholder-impact dimensions where relevant. | Confirm applicability and coverage. |
| e6 | Before deployment, the AI use case, system or model is reviewed by parties not involved in its development. | Reconcile reviewer identity/function to development ownership and deployment timing. |
| e7 | The scope and independence of the pre-deployment review are proportionate to assessed risk materiality. | Compare risk tier with review scope and independence. |
| e8 | High-risk AI receives formal independent validation before deployment by competent/objective personnel independent of development/deployment. | Conditional operating test. |
| e9 | That independent validation provides effective challenge to developers. | Look for challenge, findings, pushback and response; organisational separation alone is insufficient. |
| e10 | Independent validation covers conceptual soundness, data-input suitability/quality and implementation integrity. | Inspect validation scope and workpapers. |
| e11 | Independent validation considers evaluation measures, thresholds, testing approaches/results and relevant explainability/fairness assessments. | Reconcile validation scope to test results and applicable explainability/fairness work. |
| e12 | Review findings, limitations and conditions for use are recorded and reported to the relevant approval body before deployment. | Verify dated review record and approval-body reporting. |
| e13 | Residual risk and an accountable owner are recorded for the validation/review outcome before deployment. | Verify residual-risk statement plus named accountable owner. |
| e14 | Re-validation triggers are defined and proportionate to assessed risk materiality. | Inspect trigger/cadence criteria and risk-tier mapping. |

## Why this is better

The earlier seven-element list still contained compound elements. In particular, the testing catalogue,
validation scope, outcome recording and re-validation trigger could each hide multiple missing facts
behind one `met` verdict. The 14 atoms make those facts independently testable without falling back
to grammatical fragments.

The new contract also distinguishes **obligation** from **verification method**. Tool/plugin hints,
ToD/ToE, web knowledge and RAG can tell the engine how to test an element; they cannot silently create
new requirement elements.

## Element-generation guard

Draft generation now removes running headers and page-number spill before sentencing, requires each
model-generated element to contain meaningful action structure, checks its own anchor, rejects actor
substitution, rejects dirty anchors, caps per-sentence fan-out at three and routes failed element
quality through the inference quality gate before another model tier is tried.

The authoritative requirement remains reviewer-governed. The draft tool may propose; it may not promote
its own output.

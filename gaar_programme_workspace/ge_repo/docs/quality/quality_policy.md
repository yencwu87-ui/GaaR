# GaaR pilot quality policy

**Version 1.3 — DRAFT for approval by the governance owner.** This policy is not in force until its approval
is recorded as a signed event by a named governance owner. Until then it describes the intended rules and the
software's current behaviour; it authorises nothing.

This document is the source of truth. The machine-readable policy and the workpaper are derived from it. Where
they disagree with this document, they are wrong. A test (`tests/test_policy_consistency.py`) fails if the
decision matrix and defaults below differ from what the software enforces.

## 1. Scope

This policy governs pilot results produced on the **deterministic path**: evidence collected under a signed
standing authorisation, assessed by deterministic procedures, reconciled against a governance-signed
obligation-to-test mapping (D8), and attested by a named person. Model stages are disabled on this path.

It applies to every standing authorisation signed after its approval. The software refuses to authorise a
series on real evidence without an approval of the current policy text (`tools/gaar_policy.py approve`). Series authorised earlier keep the rules
they were signed under. The first series authorised under this policy should be the first real-data pilot, so
that the policy exists before the run it will be judged by.

## 2. The governing principle

**A gate is satisfied by a recorded, exercised demonstration. It is never satisfied by existence.**

| A gate that reads… | …is satisfied only by |
|---|---|
| Independent mapping review | A recorded review event naming the reviewer, the mapping document hash and the outcome |
| Procedure completeness | Adversarial packs actually run, and every result dispositioned |
| A refusal or integrity rule | A test that triggers the refusal and expects its exact message |
| Policy approval | A signed approval event by a named governance owner |
| A valid test run | The canonical run's record, made in an environment matching both requirements files. The environment check is part of the run's evidence, not a precondition to it |
| Collection completeness | For each period: every export arrived, was hashed at pickup, and passed the D14 and scope checks. A series with periods overdue has not "run"; it has run in part, and says which |
| Attestation quality | Recorded, not blocked: any rationale warning shown at signing is stored inside the signed attestation, and those records are reviewed |

A reviewer role that exists, packs that exist, or a guard that exists in code does not satisfy a gate.

**How each gate is held at any moment is not stated here.** This policy states rules; it is approved and pinned
by hash, so it must not carry status that events change. Gate status is reported by the gate-status report:

- it is generated by `tools/gaar_gate_status.py`, from the approvals folder, mapping reviews, the guard register,
  the canonical test and trace records, and the case journals, and never edited by hand;
- it carries a **derivation receipt**: every input it read, with its hash or ledger head, the generator's own hash,
  and the time it was generated;
- a report without a valid derivation receipt is not a gate-status report. `tools/gaar_gate_status.py verify`
  regenerates a report from the inputs its receipt names and says whether it still matches;
- status at a past moment is derived, not stored: the generator's `--as-of` evaluates only what had happened by then.

## 3. Outcomes: what the machine finds, what a person notes

Verdicts describe what the deterministic assessment found. Decisions describe what the attesting person
records. The set of decisions is **exhaustive per verdict**: exactly one epistemic situation maps to each
decision, and no decision is available under a verdict it does not describe.

| Verdict (machine) | Decisions a reviewer may sign | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ADVERSE | FAIL | A test positively found a record that contradicts an obligation |
| NO_EXCEPTIONS_FOUND | No exceptions noted for this period (assurance only) | Every obligation checked with corroborated coverage; nothing raised; not a conclusion on design or effectiveness |
| INCONCLUSIVE | FAIL (assurance only) | Assurance could not be obtained; not a finding that the control breached |

No verdict on this path permits PASS. "No exceptions noted" is offered only when every obligation reads
NO_EXCEPTIONS_FOR_PERIOD with corroborated coverage, and that is checked again at the moment of signing. A
mixed record is never softened.

The sentence the reviewer confirms names exactly what the record contains, and is stored inside the signed
attestation (see §13).

Because the matrix is exhaustive, adding a decision means adding, in one change: its full definition, the verdict
it belongs to, and its non-promotion test.

```policy
{
  "policy_version": "1.3",
  "decision_matrix": {
    "ADVERSE": [["FAIL", false]],
    "NO_EXCEPTIONS_FOUND": [["NO_EXCEPTIONS_NOTED", true]],
    "INCONCLUSIVE": [["FAIL", true]]
  },
  "defaults": {"grace_days": 2, "slack_minutes": 10},
  "model_stages_on_this_path": "disabled"
}
```

## 4. Collector contract

Every collector, present and future, inherits these rules. They are enforced at pickup, before anything is
assessed.

1. **Hash at pickup.** Each export is hashed when collected and bound to the period's signed evidence manifest.
   A later change is an integrity event (runbook E1).
2. **Scope check.** An export must declare the system and the period it belongs to. An export in the wrong
   period's folder is refused.
3. **Versioned transforms.** Any transformation from a source system's raw records to an export is itself a
   collector: versioned, hashed and named in the record.
4. **An independent population source.** Completeness is reconciled against a second, independent record of
   the population. A single-source population can never corroborate coverage.
5. **No premature completeness (D14).** An export that declares complete collection before its period has
   ended is not assessed. It is quarantined, untouched, and recorded as a signed integrity event.
6. **Partial exports never corroborate.** A partial export arriving early waits for the period to end, and a
   partial export can never make an obligation read as clean.
7. **Signed time windows.** The grace period before evidence is overdue, and the slack window for clock skew
   at a period's end, are named values in the signed standing authorisation (defaults above). They cannot be
   changed within a series. A new value means a new authorisation, which is a new series with a new
   identifier; the old series ends. Nothing about a series changes silently under the same identifier.
8. **Simulated clocks are for constructed demonstrations only**, and only where the signed authorisation marks
   the series as one. A simulated clock is recorded in the result, shown prominently, and carried into the
   signed attestation.

## 5. Coverage semantics

Findings, coverage statements and silence are three different things, and the record never lets one pass as
another. The detailed semantics are in `docs/design/coverage_reporting.md`. In summary:

- A mapped check that could not run is NOT_EVIDENCED, never silence.
- Checked 0 of 0 is NOT_EVIDENCED: an empty population usually means a broken collector.
- NO_EXCEPTIONS_FOR_PERIOD requires coverage corroborated against the independent population. It is never
  called SUPPORTED, and it is clean for the period only.
- Coverage that is not corroborated is shown as a claim, never as evidence.
- A quiet obligation with neither findings nor coverage reads "not positively shown", never clean.
- A period-scoped obligation with nothing applicable ("nothing to breach") and a standing obligation checked
  with none violated are distinguished in the record. Neither is a statement about control design.

## 6. The obligation-to-test mapping

The mapping decides which findings count against which obligation. It is a governance judgment.

1. It is signed by a named governance person, and D8 re-verifies that signature on every run.
2. It may name only finding codes the procedures actually emit. Any other code is refused.
3. An obligation without a deterministic test is allowed only with a written reason, recorded in the mapping.
4. **Independent review is a gate.** Before a series is relied on, a second person reviews the mapping and the
   review is recorded as an event naming the reviewer, the mapping document hash and the outcome
   (`tools/gaar_mapping.py review`). A review with the outcome CHANGES_REQUESTED is recorded and shown, and the
   series is not relied on until the changes are resolved. The reviewer's identity is as they declare it: the
   software can check that the reviewer's key differs from the signer's, not who either person is.

## 7. Validation of the procedures

The procedures and the constructed packs were built by the same author, so they can show that the tests catch
planted issues, never that they catch unplanned ones.

1. **Adversarial packs** are authored by a second person, from the obligation texts and the export format
   only. The author is not briefed on the code, on what the tests already detect, or on any earlier finding.
   Their value is being outside the test-authorship chain.
2. Each pack is run and each result dispositioned: a missed violation becomes a procedure defect with a
   regression case; a false positive likewise.
3. **Every refusal must be exercised.** `tools/trace_unreached_guards.py` fails if any refusal line in the
   pilot's code is unreached by tests and not in `docs/quality/unexercised_guards.md`. The register carries a
   disposition for each entry, and a registered guard is not validated until its disposition is satisfied.
4. **The canonical test run** is `tools/run_all_tests.py`. It checks the environment against the pinned
   requirements, states collected against executed, names any test file that fails to load, and lists every
   skip with its reason. Only its verdict "COMPLETE RUN, ALL PASSED" in a matching environment counts as a
   passing suite.
5. **Two claims, kept separate in every record.** A real run proves the loop works on real data. Only
   adversarial packs and dispositioned defects build the separate claim that the tests are complete.
6. **The canonical run writes a record** (`.test_runs/`): environment, requirement file hashes, counts, skips
   with reasons, failures and verdict. That record, not a remembered result, is the evidence of a test run.
   `.test_runs/` is local working storage and is not version-controlled; the record for the software version
   a series runs on is retained with that series' pilot log.

## 8. Model stages: conditional reactivation

Model qualification gates (precision and recall of at least 0.95 against independently labelled cases) do not
apply to this path, because no model output enters the record. They reactivate, in full, if a model stage
re-enters the authority path. Reactivation requires, before any such series is authorised:

1. an independently labelled corpus for the control, not authored by the procedure author. The corpus is
   **cumulative**: it must include every model failure mode already recorded (J1 to J8, with J4 recorded as
   D11) and must score attempted false assurance from the model's own receipts, not only what reached the
   record (D13);
2. a qualification report meeting the gates, signed by a named reviewer;
3. for any decision model (such as Jev or Laya), the adapter contract in the coverage design note, Appendix A:
   decisions asked only as choice questions, and an answer without a confidence field is a HOLD.

Reactivation is prospective (it governs series authorised after it) and evaluation comes first: no series runs
with a model in the authority path until the qualification report above is signed.

**Blind reading, whenever a model's output is shown to a decision-maker.** A reviewer who reads a fluent draft
before forming a view is anchored by it (Phase 0: J3 wrote cited, confident false assurance; J8 quoted a real
violation accurately and called it compliant). The rule applies to any model output shown to the person deciding,
including a decision model's typed answer with probabilities (for example Jev or Laya): structured, confident output
anchors as surely as prose. It does not apply to the deterministic path, which shows no model output.

| Substitute for a full blind reading | What it preserves | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **Evidence first** (baseline) | The reviewer's first impression forms against the deterministic findings and D8's reconciliation, which must be acknowledged before any model output is shown | The draft is still read second; residual anchoring is not measured |
| **Sampled blind readings** | Measures anchoring: a blind reading on a random 10–20% of results, plus every high-risk control, compared with the decision made after seeing the draft | Blind effort remains on the sampled share; a reduction, not a removal |

Evidence first is the minimum on every model path. Sampling is **mandatory** on any model path carrying high-risk
controls, and its measured divergence is reported; a divergence that is not measured is not assumed absent.

## 9. Vocabulary and change control

1. Changes to decision or status vocabulary apply **prospectively**. Signatures already given stand as signed.
2. **Every new decision-vocabulary item ships with its non-promotion test**, proving it cannot be sealed as a
   governance result.
3. This policy is pinned by hash in each standing authorisation signed under it. Changing the policy after
   signing stops the series (runbook U1 applies). See §12 for how changes are shown.
4. Operating rules live in `docs/operations/runbook.md`. Software messages cite the runbook; they do not
   replace it.

## 10. Independence

The pilot's validation cannot be performed by its author. Independent mapping review, adversarial pack
authorship and the review of any proposed "defensive-unreachable" guard each require a person other than the
procedure author, named in the record. For a proposed guard disposition, neither the code's owner nor the
proposer may be the reviewer; until someone outside the project reviews it, it stays "proposed — pending
external review": tracked, not validated. The software checks that keys differ; that is a proxy. The rule is about people: the person behind a key
is as declared in the signed event, which is an organisational trust, not a cryptographic one. A demonstration
in which one person holds two keys satisfies the proxy only. A first relied-upon result additionally requires that an auditor can
reproduce it without the author's help.

## 11. What this policy does not claim

- It governs the deterministic path only. It says nothing about any path with a model in authority, beyond §8.
- It does not claim the tests are complete. Adversarial packs build that claim, and none has yet been run.
- It does not certify the absence of vacuous passes beyond what the guard tracer instruments. The tracer sees
  the refusal lines in the modules it traces, and only those.
- It does not make an attestation a legal instrument. Whether a signed attestation carries legal weight is a
  matter for the organisation's own policies, not for this software.
- It does not claim that any registered guard is validated until its disposition is satisfied.

## 13. Attestation wording

The sentence a reviewer confirms enumerates exactly what was reviewed, and nothing that was not. Any change to
record content that adds a reviewable category (as coverage statements did) changes the sentence, prospectively.
The confirmed sentence and any warnings shown at signing are stored inside the signed attestation.

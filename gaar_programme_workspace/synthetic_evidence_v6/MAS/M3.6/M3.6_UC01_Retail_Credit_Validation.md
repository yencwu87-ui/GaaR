# Pre-Implementation Validation Report — PLS Risk Score v3.1

Model Risk Management | Second Line of Defence

## 1. Scope and basis of this review

This report records the pre-implementation validation of PLS Risk Score v3.1, an internally
developed gradient-boosted model producing an application risk score used as one input to the
unsecured personal loan and credit-line increase decision engine. The score does not itself
approve or decline; it feeds the existing rules-based decision matrix alongside bureau data,
affordability checks and policy rules.

Validation was performed by Model Risk Management. No member of the review team contributed to
the development, feature selection, training or tuning of v3.1. The review team comprised two
model validators and one credit risk specialist seconded from Portfolio Analytics, who was not
involved in this model's build.

Scope was determined under the Model Risk Policy (MRP-2024-03, §6.2) using the model's tier in
the retail model inventory. The model is recorded in the inventory as Tier 2. Under §6.2 a Tier 2
model receives full independent validation prior to first production use; the scope applied here
follows the Tier 2 schedule, which excludes the extended conceptual-soundness workstream and the
independent re-build required for Tier 1 models.

Materials reviewed are listed at Appendix A. Development artefacts not reproduced here are held
in the Model Governance Repository under MGR/RET-PLS-0042.

---

## 2. Model description and intended use

v3.1 replaces v2.4, which has been in production since March 2024. The principal changes are the
addition of six cashflow-derived features from the bank's transaction categorisation service, a
change of the training window from 24 to 30 months, and re-tuning of the monotonic constraints on
three bureau variables.

Intended use is limited to new unsecured personal loan applications and customer-initiated credit
line increase requests, in the Singapore retail book. The model is not approved for use in
collections, pre-approved offer generation, or any commercial lending workflow. Section 7 records
the limitations attached to this approval.

---

## 3. Data and development population

Development used applications booked between January 2023 and June 2025, with performance defined
as 90+ days past due within 12 months of origination. The development sample comprised 412,338
applications after exclusions. Exclusions and their rationale are documented in the model
development document (MDD v3.1, §4.3) and were reviewed and found reasonable.

The validator re-ran the exclusion logic against the source extract and reconciled the resulting
population to within 0.02% of the developer's count. The residual difference was traced to three
applications with amended booking dates and was accepted.

Data quality checks covered completeness, stability of the six new cashflow features over the
development window, and treatment of missing bureau records. Missing-rate drift on two of the six
new features exceeded the 5 percentage point threshold in MRP-2024-03 Appendix 4 during Q3 2024,
coinciding with a change of transaction categorisation vendor. The developer's remediation was to
re-derive those features using the current vendor's taxonomy for the full window. The validator
confirmed the re-derivation and re-ran feature stability; post-remediation drift is within
threshold.

---

## 4. Testing performed and results

### 4.1 Discrimination and calibration

Out-of-sample (20% random holdout, held by MRM and not accessible to the development team) and
out-of-time (July 2025 – March 2026) testing was performed by the validator independently of the
developer's own results.

| Test | Population | Gini | KS | Developer reported | Validator reproduced |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| In-sample | Development | 0.612 | 0.451 | 0.612 | 0.612 |
| Out-of-sample | 20% holdout | 0.589 | 0.433 | 0.591 | 0.589 |
| Out-of-time | Jul 25 – Mar 26 | 0.561 | 0.408 | 0.566 | 0.561 |

The 0.005 differences on out-of-sample and out-of-time were traced to a difference in tie-handling
in the Gini computation and are not material.

Calibration was assessed by decile against observed 12-month default. Deciles 1–8 are within the
±10% tolerance in MRP-2024-03 §7.4. Decile 9 over-predicts by 14.2% and decile 10 over-predicts by
18.6%. The developer attributes this to the low event count in the highest-score deciles. The
validator accepts the explanation but notes the consequence at §7.

### 4.2 Stability

PSI was computed for the score distribution and for each of the 23 model features, comparing the
development population to the out-of-time window. Overall score PSI is 0.043. Two features exceed
0.10 (one cashflow feature at 0.118, one bureau enquiry count at 0.104). Both are below the 0.25
action threshold.

### 4.3 Segment performance

Performance was measured across the segments defined in the Retail Credit Segmentation Standard:
new-to-bank versus existing customer, income band, and product (loan versus line increase). Gini
ranges from 0.521 (new-to-bank, lowest income band) to 0.634 (existing customer, highest income
band). The developer's documentation does not state a minimum acceptable segment-level
performance, and the Model Risk Policy does not currently specify one for Tier 2 retail models.

### 4.4 Override and edge conditions

The validator tested model behaviour on 1,200 constructed records covering boundary values,
missing bureau, zero transaction history, and out-of-range income. In 4 of 1,200 the model
returned a score outside the documented 300–850 range due to an unhandled null in a derived ratio.
This was raised as Finding V-03 and the implementation team applied a clamp; retesting confirmed
resolution.

---

## 5. Acceptance criteria

The performance thresholds applied in this review are those recorded in the Model Development
Document v3.1 §2.5, which was signed by the Model Owner on 14 April 2026. The validator notes
that MDD v3.1 §2.5 is dated after the commencement of model training (training logs in MGR show
first full training run on 2 April 2026) but before the testing recorded in §4 above. The
validator raised this sequencing as Finding V-01. The Model Owner's response states that the
criteria were agreed verbally at the model design forum on 11 March 2026 and that §2.5 records
that agreement; the minutes of the 11 March forum were not provided to the review team.

---

## 7. Conditions and limitations attached to this validation

The validation supports implementation of v3.1 for the intended use in §2, subject to:

1. V-01 and V-02 to be closed within 90 days of implementation.
2. Use restricted to the Singapore retail unsecured book. Any extension requires re-validation.
3. Scores in deciles 9–10 to be treated as indicative only for line-increase sizing until the
   calibration issue at V-04 is resolved or accepted by the Model Risk Committee.
4. Monthly calibration and PSI monitoring per the standard Tier 2 monitoring schedule.

---

## 8. Validation opinion

MRM's opinion is that PLS Risk Score v3.1 is fit for its intended use subject to the conditions
at §7. Two medium findings remain open at the date of this report.
